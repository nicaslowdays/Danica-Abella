Case Studies

Work Samples

Six pieces of work showing how I approach operations problems: finding what's wrong in financial and customer data, cleaning up records, and writing down the processes that keep it from happening again.

All samples use fabricated data. I don't publish client work, even anonymized, so every company name, figure, and record below is invented. The structure, formulas, and reasoning are the same as I'd apply to real work.
1

Billing and Revenue Audits

Problem
Invoices and signed contracts quietly drifted apart over a year. The billing system configuration looked correct, but individual invoices went out wrong.
Action
Audited 33 issued invoices line by line against contract terms, recalculating prorations, verifying price-change timing, and checking percentage-based charges.
Result
10 discrepancies, USD 2,430.73 net recoverable. Identified 8 uncollected revenue issues including unapplied escalation clauses and unbilled booking overages.
Worth noting: the most expensive errors are definitional, for example a gross rather than net commission basis. Automated checks never catch that; only reading agreements against invoices does.
2

Subscription Reconciliation

Problem
Monthly recurring revenue reports and contract records disagreed, and nobody had cross-checked them.
Action
Built a formula-driven workbook matching 37 subscription records against contracts on price, frequency, currency, dates, and discounts.
Result
19 records flagged across 8 discrepancy types, including active subscriptions with no contract and signed contracts never billed.
Worth noting: billing without a contract is a refund risk; a signed unbilled contract is uncollected revenue.
3

CRM Cleanup (for Operations Teams)

Problem
Fragmented CRM data: duplicate companies, missing job titles, and inconsistent country names such as "UK", "GB", and "United Kingdom".
Action
Profiled records, corrected data, produced an import-ready file with original values preserved, and built a full audit change log.
Result
39 field-level edits across 15 records and 6 duplicate groups. Flagged plain-text credentials for escalation.
Worth noting: I never delete records. Duplicates are proposed for merge with client review before committing.
4

Knowledge Base Restructure

Problem
34 pages in a flat list, with multiple conflicting versions of the same procedure, so staff asked in chat instead of searching.
Action
Audited every page as keep, rewrite, or retire, restructured around questions people ask, and rewrote core procedures with named owners and review dates.
Result
9 duplicates resolved, 12 pages archived, and plain-text credentials escalated for urgent removal.
Worth noting: documentation usually fails on missing thresholds, for example an approval limit nobody wrote down, not on writing quality.
5

Knowledge Base Built From Scratch

Problem
A company running on informal knowledge, with an abandoned Notion workspace nobody used.
Action
Built a three-level Notion structure organized by task, completed an ownership map, and populated critical operational procedures.
Result
3 of 10 mapped areas had no owner at all: software renewals, equipment repairs, and client data requests.
Worth noting: an ownership map reveals operational gaps an organization didn't know it had.
6

Process Documentation

Problem
Bug escalations depended entirely on one team member's knowledge, and stalled whenever they were away.
Action
Used a six-prompt asynchronous framework to capture bullet fragments from the expert in about 25 minutes, then drafted step-by-step SOPs.
Result
Numbered steps, 2 decision rules, 3 exceptions, a troubleshooting guide, and 4 unaddressed policy gaps surfaced.
Worth noting: open questions surface undocumented policy decisions so leadership can define them.

Security & Principles

How I Work

How I handle access to your systems and maintain privacy with your records.

Least Access Principle

For data work, I use exported records wherever possible. For Notion or workspace builds, I only request access to specific project pages—never full admin rights.

No Credentials Ever

I do not take shared passwords. Access is always granted via explicit user permissions that you can revoke with one click at any time.

Nothing Deleted

Superseded records and pages are archived with notes. Duplicates and structural changes are presented for approval before merging.

Findings, Not Opinions

Every audit figure traces back to raw data or contract clauses. Open questions are highlighted explicitly rather than filled in with guesses.
